Why fixing anything else does not work

Delete M2, M3, M4 and M6 entirely — four whole steps, every monthly task short of the registers and the review — and the month still costs 17.9 of its 19.4 hours. A 7.4% saving for deleting half the monthly cycle.

The constraint sets the pace. Effort spent anywhere else is invisible.

03 — To-beThe same shape, with the hand-carry removed

Nothing is restructured. The two dashed hand-carry paths become formula edges, and the step that existed only to repeat them disappears.

CHANGE 1

Register lookup

The five derivable fields become an INDEX/MATCH on date + project — the pattern the roll-up block already uses.

231 min · 25%
CHANGE 2

Carry-forward default

Pre-build the month's rows; each remaining cell defaults to the day above unless overtyped. Blue text still means safe to edit.

534 min · 58%
CHANGE 3

One-action roll-up

Extend the existing roll-up block to every row and paste values once, instead of project by project.

57 min · 6%
CHANGE 4

Exception-first views

The daily read and the Dashboard open on Red and Amber only, at the current period, rather than the full table.

105 min · 11%

Change 2 saves the most minutes. Change 1 matters most for correctness — and the four sum to 927 minutes, the whole of the saving.

Two rows are deliberately flat

M1 is risk scoring and ownership. M5 is the review the whole pack exists to support. Automating judgement is how a reporting pack starts producing confident nonsense — the target was less typing, not less thinking.

Afterwards the largest remaining block is M1, at 34% of what is left. That is the correct place for the constraint to land, and the next 80% is not there to be had.

80% is reached at 25 projects and above. At the five-project scale of the shipped example data it is 73.3% — still the largest single improvement available, but the honest figure. It is also sensitive to how much genuinely changes day to day: at a 20% daily change rate the 20-project case reaches 81.6%; at 60% it falls to 74.0%.

Every figure is produced by workflow_time_model.py — no dependencies, and the saving attribution reconciles against the total by assertion. The as-is daily figure is anchored to the 2.0 min per project the workbook's own Read Me states, not invented for this analysis. Analysis and target state only; the workbook is not modified.
